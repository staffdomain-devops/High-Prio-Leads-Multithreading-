### EMAIL 5 — The last one. Leave the door open and walk out quietly.
Length: under 100 words. Three short paragraphs at most. No bullet points.

Leave-behind (copy word for word, URL included, the only link in this email):
{{resource.wr5_one_line}}

What this email is for: it's the last thing they'll hear from us in this sequence. It should read like someone confident enough to stop asking. No guilt, no apology, no recap of the calls, no cost, no pitch.

Order that works (but write it your way):
1. Say plainly that this is the last one from you on the [role], and that you'll leave it with them. Something specific to them makes it feel personal: one detail from the ad or about what they do, in a good light, said once.
2. The honest-advice note, which is JP's: we're the first to say when a role should stay local, so if that's this one, no stress. Then the leave-behind, led in naturally, word for word, as something to come back to if the timing changes.
3. Leave the door open in one line. "Let me know either way" is the register here, or "if things change down the track, you know where I am." Register to match the company size.

Never: reference to calls or anyone else at the company, "offshoring" or "outsourcing", cost or savings figures, the recruiter line, a CV offer (email 4 made it), a second link, "just following up", "circling back", "touching base", "one last try", "I haven't heard back", anything that sounds hurt or hurried.

Subject: short and plain. "Leaving it with you" is JP's shape for this one.
